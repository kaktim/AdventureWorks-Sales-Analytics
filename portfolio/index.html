AdventureWorks Sales Analytics01  Business problem

The business problem: a wholesaler that can price nothing
and see everything

AdventureWorks sells bicycles, components and accessories to resellers in six countries. Every order is already recorded, yet the commercial team cannot answer the questions that decide next quarter: which product lines actually earn their margin, which territories are carrying the business, and whether 18,484 customers are a durable base or a one-off spike.

Why the raw workbook cannot answer them

The source is a single Excel workbook with one 60,398-row fact sheet and five dimensions. It is accurate and complete, but it is a record, not an instrument: no relationships, no measures, no calendar the facts agree with, and amounts stored per unit so that a plain SUM of the sales column silently understates revenue.

There is also no product hierarchy, no promotion or currency dimension, and no returns, budget or quota data. The report therefore has to be explicit about what it can prove and what it cannot — and it has to reconcile, to the cent, with the workbook it came from.

Where the margin is

Rank product lines and products on profit, not revenue, so the 2% line with the best margin is visible.

Where the demand is

Compare territory groups and countries on the same basis, without mixing two filter paths.

Who the buyer is

Separate one-off buyers from repeat accounts and size the value of each customer relationship.

Questions the report answers

  1. What is revenue, gross profit and margin — and what is the order value behind them?
  2. Which product line and which individual products produce the profit?
  3. How concentrated is revenue across countries and territory groups?
  4. How many buyers return, and what is a customer worth over their lifetime?
  5. Does fulfilment meet its lead-time commitment, line by line?
  6. Can any single figure be traced back to the exact order lines that produced it?
Design rule Reconcile, don’t approximate. Every page carries the calculation rule in its footer — revenue is SUMX(UnitPrice × OrderQuantity) — and the Detail page totals to the same $307,092,945.20 the KPI cards show.

Trap 1 · amounts are per unit

SalesAmount, ExtendedAmount and TotalProductCost equal the unit price and unit cost on every row; OrderQuantity (1–20) is not baked in. Tax is 8% and freight 2.5% of the unit amount. A plain SUM(SalesAmount) would understate revenue by roughly an order of magnitude, so every money measure multiplies by quantity.

Trap 2 · two date timelines

The date keys (2005-07 → 2008-07) match DimDate. The date columns are re-based to 2021-01-01 → 2022-12-31 — 2021 holds the 2005+2006 rows (3,690 lines) and 2022 holds 2007+2008 (56,708). The report uses the 2021–2022 display timeline with its own calendar and never relates the shifted columns to DimDate.

What the workbook does not contain. No product subcategory or category sheet (ProductSubcategoryKey 1–37 has nothing to join to), no promotion or currency dimension (so no currency conversion is applied), and no returns, refunds, budget or quota data. Those keys are dropped rather than given invented relationships, and no figure in this report is modelled from them.

Data model & architecture · 2 of 3

Relationships, calendar and filter architecture

Seven tables, six relationships, one marked date table, 65 measures in eight display folders. The model is embedded in the .pbix, so the report opens with no refresh prompt.

Star schema — one fact, five dimensions

Six relationships. Four run dimension → fact on the matching key — Date → OrderDate, plus ProductKey, CustomerKey and SalesTerritoryKey — so a slicer narrows the fact and never the reverse. A fifth snowflakes DimGeography into DimCustomer; the sixth is the Period Preset bridge, bidirectional only because DimDate sits on its many side. Territory never joins through Customer → Geography, so it cannot be filtered two ways.

Extract and load

The workbook is never written back to. A Python step reads it with stdlib XML streaming and writes seven curated CSVs (fact plus five dimensions and the preset bridge); each table is loaded through an M File.Contents partition, so the packaged report is self-contained. Zero-length strings are normalised to null, all-null columns (CarrierTrackingNumber, CustomerPONumber, LargePhoto, SalesTerritoryImage) and the eight duplicate-language description columns are dropped.

Date decision

The 2021–2022 display timeline is the reporting calendar. DimDate is rebuilt to run 2021-01-01 → 2022-12-31 and is marked as the date table; the raw YYYYMMDD keys are hidden. This keeps every visual, the Period slicer and the time-intelligence measures on one timeline. Customer first-purchase and product start/end dates stay unusable for time intelligence because they remain on the source timeline — they are referenced but never used to slice.

Filter bar — web parity

All five pages open with the same control strip above the KPI row. It is real, single-source filtering: the period range and the preset intersect (AND), and the reset button is Desktop’s native Clear all slicers, so no bookmark state can drift.

Why a bridge table

Calculation groups re-evaluate measures but never the rows a chart draws, so the presets are a 1,644-row (preset, date) bridge. Its relationship to DimDate must stay bidirectional: filter direction runs from the one side to the many side, and here the calendar is the many side — one-directional, picking 2021 changed nothing.

DAX measures

Measures live on the fact table and are grouped by intent, not by visual. The amount rule — unit price and unit cost multiplied by quantity — is applied once, at the base.

01 Core8 02 Customers5 03 Fulfillment4 04 Time intelligence5 05 Context4 06 Geography3 07 KPI sub-lines20 08 KPI values16
Revenue 01 Core
Revenue = SUMX ( FactInternetSales, FactInternetSales[UnitPrice] * FactInternetSales[OrderQuantity] )
Gross Profit & margin 01 Core
Gross Profit = SUMX ( FactInternetSales, ( FactInternetSales[UnitPrice] - FactInternetSales[UnitCost] ) * FactInternetSales[OrderQuantity] ) Gross Margin % = DIVIDE ( [Gross Profit], [Revenue] )
Repeat Rate 02 Customers
Repeat Customers = COUNTROWS ( FILTER ( SUMMARIZE ( FactInternetSales, FactInternetSales[CustomerKey], "OrderCount", DISTINCTCOUNT ( FactInternetSales[SalesOrderNumber] ) ), [OrderCount] >= 2 ) ) Repeat Rate = DIVIDE ( [Repeat Customers], [Customers] )
Late Line Rate — zero-safe 03 Fulfillment
Late Lines = CALCULATE ( COUNTROWS ( FactInternetSales ), KEEPFILTERS ( FactInternetSales[LateDays] > 0 ) ) + 0 Late Line Rate = DIVIDE ( [Late Lines], [Lines], 0 )
KPI Revenue — one formatting pattern for every money card 08 KPI values
// a text measure: the card's display units turned 27,659 into "28K" KPI Revenue = VAR v = [Revenue] VAR a = ABS ( v ) RETURN "$" & SWITCH ( TRUE (), a >= 1000000000, FORMAT ( v / 1000000000, "0.00" ) & "B", a >= 1000000, FORMAT ( v / 1000000, "0.00" ) & "M", a >= 1000, FORMAT ( v / 1000, "0.0" ) & "K", FORMAT ( ROUND ( v, 0 ), "#,##0" ) )

Three deliberate choices

1. SUMX, never SUM. Quantity is part of the amount, so it is applied inside the iterator; cost and freight follow the same rule, which is why margin is 41.12%.

2. +0 on empty results. Every LateDays value is zero, so the card shows 0.00% and 0 — the truth, not a blank that looks like a formatting error.

AdventureWorks Sales Analytics06  Technical skills
How it was built

Technical skills demonstrated

Everything below was done from a single read-only workbook, with no database, no external service and no manual refresh between captures.

Data modelling & preparation

  • Star schema — one fact and five dimensions, six relationships with explicit cardinality, and a grain (order + line) proven unique across 60,398 rows.
  • Date-table discipline — a dedicated 2021–2022 calendar marked as the date table, with raw YYYYMMDD keys hidden so no visual can slice on the wrong field.
  • Filter-direction design — diagnosing why a single-direction relationship silently disabled the period presets, and keeping the bridge bidirectional on purpose.
  • Power Query (M) — File.Contents partitions, type enforcement, zero-length strings normalised to null, all-null and duplicate-language columns pruned.
  • Source discipline — extract to curated CSVs, never write back, and drop keys that have no dimension rather than inventing a join.
  • Extraction pipeline — Python parses the workbook with stdlib zip/XML streaming and emits seven curated CSVs: one per table plus the preset bridge.

DAX & analytical logic

  • Iterator-based aggregation — SUMX over unit price and unit cost, because quantity is not baked into the amount columns.
  • Safe ratios — DIVIDE with an alternate result everywhere a denominator can be empty; margin, AOV and rates never divide by zero.
  • Zero-safe counting — KEEPFILTERS plus + 0 so a genuinely empty result reads 0, not blank.
  • Time intelligence — SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR for prior-year revenue, profit and margin, with the change expressed in percentage points.
  • Context and formatting — DISTINCTCOUNT, SUMMARIZE, FILTER, SELECTEDVALUE, and SWITCH-based text measures that control exactly how a card reads.
  • Measure architecture — 65 measures in eight display folders, where text KPIs reuse the base measures so a formatting change can never fork the logic.

Report, engineering & QA

  • Report design — five-page PBIR layout, a shared filter bar on every page, layered KPI cards, and a provenance footer that states source, rule, timeline and snapshot.
  • Interaction design — slicer actions (ClearAllSlicers), page navigation, cross-filtering, and a Detail explorer that reconciles to the summary cards.
  • Code-generated assets — Python generators for the TMDL model and PBIR pages, plus a field-reference check that validates every visual against the model.
  • Reconciliation testing — control totals recomputed from source, model and rendering, 10/10 integrity checks, and the amount rule re-tested on every rebuild.
  • Evidence capture — full-resolution captures of the current Power BI Desktop report pages, cropped to the report canvas.
  • Documentation — a README recording the page map, control totals, rebuild steps and every known limitation, so another analyst can audit or rebuild the model.
Toolkit  Power BI Desktop · Power Query (M) · DAX · PBIP (TMDL + PBIR) · Python 3.13 (stdlib XML/zip parsing) · Excel · Azure SQL Database/T-SQL reference integration · Edge for PDF authoring.
What that adds up to a model that cannot silently misstate revenue, a report a non-technical reader can operate unaided, and a documented trail from the source row to the number on the screen.

Limitations — verified against the workbook
  • Per-unit amounts. Any new measure must multiply by OrderQuantity; SUM(SalesAmount) is never used.
  • Two timelines. Customer first-purchase and product start/end dates stay on the source calendar and are not used for time intelligence.
  • No product hierarchy. Subcategory and category names are not recoverable from this workbook, so product analysis stops at the line and product.
  • No promotion or currency dimension. Keys are dropped; all amounts are in one reporting currency with no conversion applied.
  • No returns, budget or quota. Shipped sales only — no target variance.
  • Territory is reachable twice. The fact key drives territory visuals and the customer dimension drives country visuals; mixing them in one visual would create an ambiguous path.
  • Dimension coverage. Only 158 of 606 products sell, and territory 11 exists but is never used, so counting the catalogue requires an outer join.
The tie-out

$307,092,945.20 on the Overview card, $307,092,945.20 in the Detail footer, and the same figure when the extract is recomputed from the workbook — three independent paths to one number. That is the standard every figure in this report is held to.

01

Keep the grain

Every new page stays at one row per order line — SalesOrderNumber plus SalesOrderLineNumber. Because revenue is a SUMX over quantity, a table that fans the fact out would inflate $307.09M without raising a single error.

02

Isolate the targets

A budget table must reach the fact through the territory and calendar dimensions, never directly. Joined at line level the quota is multiplied by the number of orders, and every variance figure becomes confidently wrong.

03

Reuse the path

Territory is reachable from the fact and again via customer → geography. Pick one per visual. Enabling both reintroduces the ambiguous filter path this model was built to remove.

The model is finished and reconciled. What it needs next is richer context — hierarchy, targets, security — not repair.
